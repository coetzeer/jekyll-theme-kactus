I walked the quays at seven. The Liffey still held the night's ink. The Samuel Beckett Bridge harped its cables in the easterly. No mist today. Met Éireann promised clear spells and kept its word, rare as a politician's promise. The air tasted of salt and warmed stone, the particular perfume of a Dublin August that has forgotten rain.

Yesterday the city thrummed with the Fleadh. Five hubs they called them — Smithfield, Capel Street, Wolfe Street Square, Parliament Street, Meeting House Square — as if music could be zoned like planning permission. The Scratch and Madra Salach. Sharon Shannon's big band. Kíla. Altan. Andy Irvine with Quilty. Dervish. Máirtín O'Connor's trio. I didn't go. I never go. But I heard it bleed through the walls of the flat on Capel Street. A fiddle reel slipping under the sash window while I made tea. The bodhrán's heartbeat syncing with my own. Music in this city doesn't ask permission. It seeps.

A man on the Ha'penny Bridge played "The Lark in the Morning" on a tin whistle. Notes bright as the gulls above. Tourists tossed coins. A woman in a hi-vis vest from the SportsFest launch — ten years they're celebrating, ten years of getting Dublin moving — paused, listened, checked her watch, moved on. The tune outlasted her schedule.

The bond markets stir in their glass towers on the docklands. Ireland's ten-year yield climbed to three-point-four-two percent last Friday. The NTMA boasting they've raised seventy percent of their target. Eleven-and-a-half billion repaid and gone. Numbers ghosting through fiber while the whistle plays on. I think of my father checking the Irish Independent's financial pages at the kitchen table, the same furrow between his brows. The furrow remains. The paper does not.

Thomas Davis won in Tallaght. Senior football championship. The GAA calendar ticks on. Minor hurlers in Kinnegad. Minor footballers in Parnell Park. The fixtures calendar confirmed by the CCC. The great machine of parish and county grinding its reliable gears. In a Garda station in Dublin 24, a man in his twenties sits under Section 4. Drug-related intimidation. The shadow trade that fuels the bright city.

I stop at the newsagent on Aston Quay. The Irish Times. The Independent. The Star. The Mail. Front pages promising crime and weather and Charlie being serious about marrying this woman. I buy nothing. The headlines will be different tomorrow. The bridge will still harp. The Liffey will still carry its brown silence to the bay.

A swan lifts from the water near the Custom House. Wings beating the heavy air. Not graceful. Necessary. The easterly freshens. Somewhere in Smithfield, a fiddle case snaps shut. The Fleadh is done. The music remains, humming in the bones of the streets, waiting for the next pair of hands to wake it.

I turn for home. The light has shifted, hazier now as promised, gilding the spires and the cranes alike. Another Monday. The city breathes. I breathe with it.
