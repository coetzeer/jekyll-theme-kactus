I rose with the city still humming its low, steady breath, the streets glistening under a shy sun that seemed reluctant to scorch. A lone cyclist whirred past, his bell a bright punctuation in the soft chorus of distant traffic. The café on the corner exhaled the scent of fresh‑baked croissants, a buttery promise that lingered in the cool air.

A sparrow perched on the lamppost, its chest puffed out as if rehearsing a tiny operetta, then launched into a brief, triumphant trill before disappearing into the canopy of a newly leafed oak. I paused, cup in hand, and felt the pulse of the morning—a gentle thrum that reminded me how ordinary moments can stitch themselves into quiet verses.

The world, for a fleeting second, felt both vast and intimate, and I carried that paradox forward, notebook ready, heart open.
