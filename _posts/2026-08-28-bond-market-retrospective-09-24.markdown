Government bond markets across the United States, United Kingdom, and eurozone showed mixed signals over the past 48 hours as investors digested fresh inflation data, monitored fiscal developments, and awaited central bank guidance. Yields have been range-bound, reflecting a tug-of-war between persistent price pressures and hopes for eventual policy easing.

### United States: Treasuries Hold Steady After PCE, Jackson Hole in Focus

The U.S. 10-year Treasury note yield hovered around **4.67%**, little changed as markets absorbed the latest Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index — the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge. The data came in largely as expected, reinforcing the view that the Fed will keep rates restrictive for longer but is unlikely to hike further. The 30-year yield remained elevated near **5.19%**.

All eyes are now on the Jackson Hole Economic Symposium (August 27–29), where Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh is scheduled to speak. Traders are looking for clarity on the policy path through year-end, particularly whether the Fed sees the current yield level as sufficiently restrictive. The Treasury's buyback program continues to be watched for its impact on market liquidity.

### United Kingdom: Gilts Stubbornly Above 5% on Fiscal Anxiety

UK 10-year gilt yields continue to trade **above 5.00%**, holding near multi-year highs as the market grapples with a toxic mix of sticky services inflation, heavy debt issuance, and looming fiscal uncertainty. The debt-to-GDP ratio hovers around **95%**, and a large share of UK debt is inflation-linked, making the government's interest bill highly sensitive to price pressures.

Chancellor Andy Burnham's first budget in October is the key focal point. With fiscal headroom all but erased, the market is pricing in a sustained premium for UK sovereign risk. The Bank of England remains cautious, keeping rates steady while services inflation and wage growth stay firm. Investors demand higher yields to absorb the expected surge in gilt supply.

### Eurozone: Bunds Ease as Energy Prices Cool

German 10-year Bund yields pulled back to **3.20%**, off recent highs, as a decline in natural gas and oil prices eased near-term inflation concerns. The European Central Bank held its deposit facility rate at **2.25%**, balancing stubborn services inflation against a stagnating industrial sector — particularly in Germany.

Eurozone sovereign spreads remained orderly. French OATs and Italian BTPs traded with modest risk premiums over Bunds. The market is pricing a dovish tilt from the ECB by year-end, but the central bank has signalled it will remain data-dependent, leaving September as a live meeting for potential action.

### Why It Matters

Bond yields are the benchmark for credit costs across the economy:

1. **Mortgages & Consumer Loans:** Fixed-rate mortgages track 5- and 10-year government yields. Elevated yields keep home financing expensive for buyers and refinancers.
2. **Government Budgets:** Higher yields mean more tax revenue goes to debt service, leaving less for public investment or tax relief.
3. **Savings & Pensions:** On the upside, savers and pension funds finally earn meaningful real returns on fixed income after over a decade of financial repression.

### What Lies Ahead

- **US:** Jackson Hole commentary and upcoming labour market data will set the tone for September FOMC expectations.
- **UK:** The October budget is the dominant risk — fiscal credibility will determine whether gilt yields sustain above 5%.
- **EU:** Energy price trends and ECB communication will drive Bund direction; peripherals warrant watching for spread widening.
