The light falls differently in October. Not the sharp white of July that flattens everything, not the grey soup of November that presses against the windowpanes. This morning it comes pale gold, thin as tea, slipping between the chimneys of Rathmines and catching the damp on the cobblestones. Fourteen degrees. The wind moves at twelve kilometres an hour from the southwest — I know this because the weather service tells me, but my face tells me the same thing in a different language. Clear sky, mainly. The kind of morning that makes you believe the day might be kind.

I walked past the former Rathmines Post Office on my way to the shop. The building sits there still, red brick and limestone, the way it has since 1934. Ninety-two years of stamps and pensions and registered letters and the quiet rustle of official envelopes. Closed in July. Reopened in a Centra on Lower Rathmines Road — a Centra, imagine that, the smell of baked goods and plastic-wrapped sandwiches where the postal scales used to be. Two thousand five hundred signatures on a petition. A one-year rolling licence granted Wednesday, effective after the twenty-eighth. An Post still intends to sell in 2027. Ivana Bacik calls it a watershed moment. I call it a stay of execution. The building breathes. It remembers every hand that pushed through its doors. The weight of that memory presses against the new licence like a ghost leaning on a shoulder.

The M50 was a car park by seven. Collision on the N3 southbound between Junction 6 Dunshaughlin and Junction 5 Dunboyne. Services en route. Traffic building behind it. The southbound M50 heavily congested from Junction 4 Ballymun to after Junction 12 Firhouse. Northbound slow around Junction 7 Lucan. The Swords Road backed up to the Dublin Tunnel. The Navan Road full back to the Circle K at Ashtown Roundabout. Phibsborough Road inbound backed up before Harts Corner. Longmile Road to Walkinstown Avenue crawling. Drimnagh Road heavy. Ballinteer Road delays. Clanbrassil Street and Patrick Street full. A city held hostage by metal and momentum. I heard the horns from three streets away — not angry, just tired. The sound of a thousand people who left home too early and will arrive too late. The radio in the kitchen gave the updates every ten minutes like a rosary. *Collision on N3. Heavy on the M50. Delays on the Swords Road.* The words accumulate. They become a litany.

In the midst of it all, the city prepares for Oktoberfest. The Circular in Rialto morphs into a beer hall this weekend — giant Swabien-style pretzels, currywurst, half-litre steins for fifteen euro. Priory Market in Tallaght does three days from the twenty-fourth: bratwurst from the BBQ, Weissbier and Helles and Bock and Pils brewed on site, served in litre steins. Rascals hosts Rocktoberfest on October tenth — German lager, Irish craft, rock and punk and emo classics. The Bloody Stream in Howth. The Bernard Shaw in Phibsboro. Keepers of the Bavarian flame, the article calls them. I think of the flags and bunting going up, the communal tables, the banter. Prost. The word tastes of honey and hops. There is something stubbornly alive in a city that can still build a beer hall in the shadow of a traffic jam and a post office fight.

The budget looms. October sixth. Jack Chambers says a few hundred euro in tax cuts for workers. Darragh Cassidy from Bonkers.ie says households face up to a thousand euro in new costs. One hundred forty more for gas. One hundred eighty for electricity. VHI up two point seven five percent — two to three hundred extra for cover. Tracker mortgages rising, some by hundreds. ESB interest rate hikes — four hundred more a year on a hundred and fifty thousand mortgage over ten to fifteen years. Public transport up fifteen percent in January. PRSI increasing next month. Carbon tax maybe twenty more for the average driver. Streaming services creeping up. The arithmetic is brutal. A few hundred given with one hand, a thousand taken with the other. Mary Lou McDonald in the Dáil: *You and Simon Harris simply cannot come in here on October 6 to roll out yet another budget that fails to tackle the cost of living.* Chambers: *A significant level of uncertainty with global conflicts.* The language of insulation. We can insulate to some extent. But not all elements. The words float away like steam from a cup.

I stand at the window now. The light has deepened. A plane traces a white line across the pale blue. Somewhere on the N3 they're clearing the collision. Somewhere in Rathmines a petition signature dries on a page. Somewhere in Rialto a pretzel dough rises. Somewhere a family does the sums at the kitchen table — gas, electric, insurance, mortgage, transport, the small luxuries that make a life bearable. The light doesn't care. It falls on the cobblestones and the red brick and the traffic and the steins and the kitchen tables equally. Fourteen degrees. Clear sky mainly. The wind at twelve kilometres an hour. The day begins.

*Prost*, I say to the empty room. To the post office that refuses to die. To the drivers on the M50. To the arithmetic that never balances. To the light, thin as tea, that makes it all visible for a moment.
